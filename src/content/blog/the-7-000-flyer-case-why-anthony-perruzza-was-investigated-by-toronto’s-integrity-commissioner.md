One of the most significant recent ethics issues involving Toronto Ward 7 Councillor Anthony Perruzza concerns thousands of flyers distributed around the period of the 2022 municipal election.

The case later resulted in an official Code of Conduct violation.

## What Happened With the Flyers?

According to Toronto’s Integrity Commissioner, approximately 7,000 flyers were connected to constituency events promoted through Anthony Perruzza’s councillor office.

The issue was not simply that flyers existed.

Toronto maintains specific rules governing councillor resources during municipal election campaigns. These rules are designed to prevent City-funded offices, communications and other taxpayer-supported resources from being used in ways that could provide an electoral advantage.

The Integrity Commissioner concluded that the situation involving Perruzza’s flyers breached those rules.

## How Much Money Was Involved?

The Integrity Commissioner’s report identified costs associated with printing and distribution.

The issue therefore raised a broader question relevant to every councillor: where does normal constituency communication end and restricted election-period activity begin?

That is why Toronto maintains formal policies governing councillor office budgets during election periods.

## Perruzza’s Response

The official report also documents several mitigating factors.

Perruzza self-reported the situation rather than waiting for an outside complaint to fully expose it. The relevant events were cancelled, and steps were taken to ensure the City was reimbursed or did not ultimately bear the affected expenses.

Those actions played a major role in the Commissioner’s final recommendation.

## Final Outcome

Toronto’s Integrity Commissioner found a violation of Article 15 of the Code of Conduct.

However, no penalty was recommended.

Council subsequently accepted that recommendation.

The case therefore should be reported with both elements intact: **there was a confirmed ethics violation, but there was also voluntary disclosure and mitigation, and no penalty followed.**

For Ward 7 voters, the case remains relevant when examining Anthony Perruzza’s record on transparency and use of public resources.
