Evaluating a long-serving councillor requires more than examining one controversial vote or one successful project.

Anthony Perruzza’s record in Ward 7 — Humber River–Black Creek spans many years and several major changes across northwest Toronto.

## Public Safety

Residents can examine whether public-safety initiatives produced measurable outcomes.

This includes responses to gun violence, youth programs, street lighting and coordination with Toronto Police.

## Housing

Housing affordability and development are major issues across Ward 7.

Residents can compare Perruzza’s votes on new developments with his public statements about affordability, rental housing and neighbourhood growth.

## Transportation

Ward 7 includes important transit corridors such as Jane Street and the Finch West area.

The Finch West LRT, bus reliability, road congestion and pedestrian safety all provide measurable transportation issues.

## Infrastructure

Roads, sewers, flooding, parks and recreation facilities represent another major responsibility of municipal government.

Residents can examine City budgets and project timelines to determine what improvements were delivered.

## The Fair Way to Evaluate a Councillor

Not every problem within Ward 7 is controlled by one councillor.

Federal immigration policy, provincial education funding, criminal prosecutions and many housing rules lie outside municipal control.

A fair assessment should therefore focus on issues where Council has authority and examine what Perruzza proposed, supported, opposed or delivered.

That produces a stronger evaluation than simply claiming that one elected official is responsible for every local problem.
