Toronto needs significantly more housing, but almost every large development generates difficult questions about density, traffic, infrastructure and existing tenants.

Anthony Perruzza’s voting record provides several examples of how these competing priorities play out in Ward 7.

## Weston Road

At 3406–3434 Weston Road, Perruzza attempted to have the development application refused in its existing form.

Council rejected his motion and ultimately moved forward with planning approvals.

## Jane Street

Another major planning case involved 2775 Jane Street.

The project included hundreds of new homes and protections related to existing rental housing.

Perruzza did not support the final Council position.

## What Does This Tell Residents?

Two votes are not enough to prove that a councillor is either “pro-housing” or “anti-housing.”

Individual projects contain different planning circumstances.

However, examining multiple development votes can reveal patterns.

Residents may ask whether Perruzza generally favours more neighbourhood consultation, lower density, stronger tenant protections or tighter development conditions.

Those are legitimate policy questions as Toronto attempts to address its housing shortage.
