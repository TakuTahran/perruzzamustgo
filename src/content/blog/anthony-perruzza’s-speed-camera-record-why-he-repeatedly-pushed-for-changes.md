Anthony Perruzza’s October 2025 attempt to pause speed-camera penalties was part of a broader pattern of concern about Toronto’s Automated Speed Enforcement system.

Earlier in 2025, he had already pushed Council to examine changes to the program.

## Concerns About Multiple Tickets

One area of debate involved situations where a driver could potentially receive several camera-generated penalties before the first ticket arrived.

Perruzza sought greater review of how such cases were handled.

## Camera Visibility and Signage

He also raised questions concerning signage and the visibility of speed cameras.

Supporters of highly visible cameras often argue that the primary objective should be deterrence rather than maximizing the number of tickets issued.

Others argue that drivers are responsible for obeying posted speed limits regardless of whether a camera is clearly visible.

## The Larger Vision Zero Debate

Toronto’s Vision Zero strategy is intended to reduce serious injuries and deaths on city streets.

Automated enforcement is one of the tools Toronto uses to discourage speeding.

Perruzza’s amendments therefore placed him in a broader debate about how aggressive enforcement should be and how much emphasis should be placed on warnings and notification.

Residents evaluating his record should examine both sides rather than reducing the issue to “for” or “against” road safety.
