For many households, getting ahead is not only about finding a job.

It can also depend on whether residents can afford the transit needed to get to work, access training, pay for childcare or participate in programs that help young people build skills.

In Ward 7, Humber River–Black Creek, these issues are closely connected.

The City of Toronto cannot control every factor affecting wages or the cost of living, but it does have tools that can connect residents with employment, training and services designed to reduce some household expenses.

The practical question is:

**Are those opportunities reaching the residents who need them?**

## Connecting Residents With Employment Support

Toronto Employment & Social Services provides financial and social supports and can connect eligible residents, particularly people receiving Ontario Works, with employment and training services.

Since Toronto moved into Ontario's Integrated Employment Services system in 2025, Employment Ontario delivers many employment services, while Toronto Employment & Social Services helps clients address barriers and connects them with appropriate employment, training and community supports.

For residents, this can include help finding:

Employment services

Skills training

Education programs

Career preparation

Community supports

Childcare or other services that may make employment possible

The important issue is not simply whether a program exists.

Residents also need to know where to find it and whether they qualify.

## Ward 7 Has a Local Employment & Social Services Location

Ward 7 residents have access to the City's **Yorkgate Employment & Social Services** location at:

**1 York Gate Blvd., Toronto**

The office provides financial and social supports and referrals to employment services for Toronto residents receiving Ontario Works.

Current City information lists phone and in-person service from Monday through Friday.

The City has also continued offering Ontario Works information sessions through the Yorkgate Intake & Access Centre, including information about employment services, training, benefits and other supports.

Having services available locally can reduce one barrier.

The next question is whether residents know those services exist and can navigate them easily.

## Training Should Connect to Real Employment Opportunities

Training is most useful when it leads toward skills employers actually need.

Toronto currently provides or connects residents with training opportunities including employment readiness, First Aid, Food Handler Certification, small-business training and other skills programs.

Residents receiving Ontario Works may also be referred to Employment Ontario for additional employment and skills training.

A useful local workforce strategy should therefore ask:

**What jobs are available?**

**What qualifications do employers require?**

**What training helps residents qualify?**

**How quickly can someone complete that training?**

**Is the training affordable?**

**Does it lead to an interview, placement or recognized certification?**

Simply counting the number of workshops offered does not show whether residents are finding sustainable employment afterward.

## More Local Hiring Opportunities

The Jane Finch Community Development Plan specifically addresses **Inclusive Employment Opportunities**.

Among the actions identified in the plan are exploring equity-focused community-benefit opportunities related to employment and procurement and supporting community economic-development efforts associated with redevelopment projects such as Firgrove.

This creates an important question whenever public investment or major development reaches the area:

**Can local residents benefit from the employment created by that investment?**

Possible mechanisms can include local recruitment, training connections, apprenticeships and community-benefit arrangements where those are part of an approved project.

But those opportunities should be tracked with actual numbers.

How many positions became available?

How many local residents applied?

How many received training?

How many were hired?

How long did those jobs last?

Those measurements provide a clearer picture of whether an employment initiative is working.

## Youth Employment Needs a Clear Pathway

Young people can face a familiar problem:

Employers want experience, but young workers need their first job in order to gain experience.

Toronto currently operates several youth employment initiatives.

The **Youth Employment Partnerships Network** connects young people aged 15 to 29 with employment, training, education, entrepreneurship and skills-development opportunities.

The City also operates **Toronto Youth Partnerships & Employment**, or TYPE, for young people aged 16 to 29 facing significant barriers to employment.

Toronto's **Building Skills through Recreation** program also offers free employment-related skills and certifications for youth aged 13 to 24 in areas including recreation, sports, aquatics, cycling and community leadership.

Programs like these can help create a pathway:

**Training → Certification → Work experience → Employment**

The useful measure is how many participants successfully move through that pathway.

## Support Local Entrepreneurs Too

Employment does not only mean working for an existing employer.

Some residents want to create their own jobs by starting businesses.

The Jane Finch Community Development Plan includes a separate action area for **Inclusive Entrepreneurship Opportunities**.

The plan calls for expanded Toronto Entrepreneurship Services programming in Jane Finch, partnerships providing entrepreneurship support and mentorship, and connections between local entrepreneurs and business-incubation spaces.

For local entrepreneurs, useful supports can include:

Business planning

Mentorship

Market research

Digital skills

Licensing information

Financing guidance

Business-incubator access

Procurement opportunities

Affordable commercial space

The important question is whether local residents can easily find and use those services.
