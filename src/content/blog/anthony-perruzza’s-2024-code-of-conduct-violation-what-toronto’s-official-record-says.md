Anthony Perruzza, the Toronto City Councillor representing Ward 7 — Humber River–Black Creek, became the subject of an official Integrity Commissioner investigation concerning the use of City resources during the 2022 municipal election period.

The matter eventually resulted in a formal finding that Perruzza had violated Article 15 of Toronto’s Code of Conduct for Members of Council.

## What Was the Issue?

The investigation concerned constituency-event flyers associated with Perruzza’s councillor office. Toronto’s election-period rules place restrictions on how City-funded resources may be used once the restricted election period begins.

According to the Integrity Commissioner’s report, approximately 7,000 flyers had been produced and distributed in connection with community events during the restricted period.

The Commissioner concluded that the use of City resources in this situation did not comply with Article 15.

That makes this different from a simple political disagreement or controversial vote. It was an **official Code of Conduct finding**.

## Why Was No Penalty Recommended?

An important part of the record is what happened after the issue was identified.

Perruzza reported the matter himself, cancelled the affected events and took steps to reimburse or prevent City funds from ultimately covering the relevant costs.

The Integrity Commissioner considered those actions when determining what should happen next.

Although a violation was found, the Commissioner recommended that Toronto City Council impose **no penalty**.

Council later accepted both the violation finding and the recommendation against a penalty.

## Why This Matters for Ward 7 Residents

For voters in Humber River–Black Creek, the case provides a concrete example of how Toronto’s ethics and election-period rules apply to councillors.

It also demonstrates why context is important.

The accurate description is that **Anthony Perruzza was found to have violated Article 15 of Toronto’s Code of Conduct, but no penalty was recommended because he self-reported and mitigated the matter.**

Describing it as a criminal offence or suggesting that Perruzza was convicted of corruption would go beyond the evidence contained in the official record.

For residents evaluating Anthony Perruzza’s record, the incident remains a legitimate issue of political accountability, particularly because it involved the use of public resources during an election period.
