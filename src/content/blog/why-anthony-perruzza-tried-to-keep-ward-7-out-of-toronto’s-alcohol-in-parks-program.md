Toronto’s decision to allow alcohol consumption in selected public parks generated considerable debate across the city.

When Council considered making the program permanent, Anthony Perruzza attempted to keep Ward 7 parks outside the program.

## Perruzza’s Motion

Perruzza moved an amendment directing that no park located in Ward 7 — Humber River–Black Creek — should be designated for personal alcohol consumption.

The motion was defeated by Council.

Toronto subsequently continued with a broader framework allowing alcohol consumption in designated parks.

## Why Councillors Disagreed

Supporters of the program argued that Toronto residents should be able to responsibly consume alcohol in suitable public parks, particularly because many residents live in apartments without private outdoor space.

Opponents raised concerns about noise, public disorder, family use of parks and enforcement.

Perruzza clearly aligned himself with the more restrictive position for Ward 7.

## What Ward 7 Residents Can Evaluate

The vote provides a straightforward public record.

Residents can decide whether they believe Perruzza was appropriately reflecting local concerns or whether Ward 7 should have participated alongside other Toronto neighbourhoods.

It is also important to recognize that this was a policy disagreement—not an ethics issue or scandal.
