Toronto’s Automated Speed Enforcement program has become one of the City’s most visible road-safety initiatives.

In 2025, Ward 7 Councillor Anthony Perruzza proposed changes that would have temporarily paused certain speed-camera penalties while aspects of the system were reviewed.

## What Did Perruzza Propose?

Perruzza’s proposal called for penalties to be paused while drivers received warnings during a review period.

The issues he wanted examined included camera visibility, signage, repeated tickets, enforcement hours and situations where drivers could receive multiple penalties before becoming aware of the first violation.

## Council Rejected the Motion

The proposal did not receive enough support from Toronto City Council.

That left the broader Automated Speed Enforcement system in place while Council considered other program modifications.

## Why Was the Motion Controversial?

Supporters of automated speed enforcement argue that cameras reduce dangerous speeding and help protect pedestrians, children and cyclists.

Critics sometimes argue that enforcement needs clearer signage and protections against situations where motorists accumulate multiple penalties before receiving initial notice.

Perruzza’s proposal placed him on the side seeking significant changes to enforcement.

## What the Vote Does and Does Not Prove

The vote establishes Perruzza’s policy position.

It does not prove that he opposes road safety.

Likewise, opposition to his amendment does not automatically establish that other councillors favour unfair enforcement.

The real debate concerns how Toronto should combine deterrence, transparency and procedural fairness.

For Ward 7 residents, the vote is useful when evaluating where Perruzza stands on traffic enforcement and Vision Zero-related policies.
