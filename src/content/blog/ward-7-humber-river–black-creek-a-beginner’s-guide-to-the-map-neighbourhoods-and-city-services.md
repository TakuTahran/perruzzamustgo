A Beginner’s Guide to Ward 7 Humber River–Black Creek

**Ward 7, officially called Humber River–Black Creek, is one of Toronto’s 25 municipal wards.** It elects one councillor to represent the area at City Council. If you are new to the ward, the most useful place to start is with your address: Toronto’s [official Ward 7 map](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/8d0d-CityPlanning-Map-Ward-7.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com) shows the electoral boundary, while the City’s [ward profile](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-7-humber-river-black-creek/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) brings together local data and public notices. 

## Where is Ward 7?

Ward 7 is in northwest Toronto. Places associated with the ward include Jane and Finch and Humber Summit, along with other residential and employment areas. A familiar neighbourhood name, however, is not a reliable way to check an electoral boundary. A local plan or community service area may also extend beyond one ward.

For an address-specific answer, enter your Toronto address into the City’s [MyVote tool](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/voter-information/myvote/?utm_source=chatgpt.com). It can show your ward on a map and identify the candidates running there. The City advises users to select their address from the tool’s dropdown menu so it can match the location correctly. 

## What can the City’s ward profile tell you?

The [Humber River–Black Creek ward profile](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/data-research-maps/neighbourhoods-communities/ward-profiles/ward-7-humber-river-black-creek/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) links to a profile based on the **2021 Census**. That is a useful starting point for learning about the ward’s population and housing. Because the figures describe 2021, they should be read alongside newer information when discussing current conditions. 

The same City page also publishes construction notices organized by street. If roadwork affects your trip, check the notice for the particular street and its **latest update date**. An earlier construction notice may have been followed by a revised schedule or a deferral. 

## Why does the exact location matter?

“Jane and Finch” is a useful way to describe a community, but it should not replace a street address or a project boundary in a local news report. For example, the City’s [Jane Finch Community Development Plan](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2024/ec/bgrd/backgroundfile-242910.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com) concerns **both Ward 7 and neighbouring Ward 6**. If an article is about a proposed building, crossing or community facility, naming its location helps readers understand who is affected and which public records apply. 

## Who should residents contact about a local problem?

The right contact depends on the issue. The City’s Ward 7 profile provides access to the [councillor’s office](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/council/members-of-council/councillor-ward-7/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), construction notices and apartment-building information. Residents can use **311** to ask about City services or report many maintenance concerns. For a particular problem, record the address or nearest intersection, the date and any service-request number. Those details make a follow-up easier to track. 

Ward 7 is easier to understand one location and one issue at a time. Check your address against the official map, use dated City notices for current projects, and keep the relevant record when raising a concern. That gives you a clear starting point for following decisions that affect your part of Humber River–Black Creek.
