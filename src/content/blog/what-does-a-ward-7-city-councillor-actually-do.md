WHAT DOES A WARD 7 CITY COUNCILLOR ACTUALLY DO?

![](/images/uploads/whatsapp-image-2026-09-24-at-2.37.12-am.jpeg)

A Ward 7 city councillor represents Humber River–Black Creek residents at Toronto City Council. They listen to local concerns, take part in Council decisions and work with City staff on issues affecting the ward.

But what does that mean when a resident needs help with a damaged sidewalk, an apartment maintenance problem or a bus route?

REPRESENTING RESIDENTS

Residents can contact the councillor’s office about City services, neighbourhood projects and decisions coming before Council. A useful message explains the problem, gives the exact address or intersection and says how residents are affected.

If you have already reported the issue to the City, include your service-request number. That gives the councillor’s office a clear record to follow up on.

TAKING PART IN CITY DECISIONS

Councillors can raise proposals, discuss recommendations and vote at Council and committee meetings. They also hear from residents, businesses and community groups.

One councillor cannot guarantee that a project will be approved or completed by a particular date. A proposal may need a staff report, funding, a Council decision or action by another agency. When you hear about a new plan, ask: What has actually been approved, and what is the next step?

HELPING RESIDENTS FIND THE RIGHT SERVICE

For many City maintenance problems, 311 Toronto is the place to start. Residents can submit a request and use the reference number to track its status. A TTC bus or subway concern should also be reported to the TTC.

The councillor can raise a continuing issue or ask for information, but the City department or transit agency responsible for the service handles the work. Reporting the problem directly creates a record that you can use when following up.

![](/images/uploads/whatsapp-image-2026-09-24-at-2.37.23-am.jpeg)

HOW CAN YOU HAVE A SAY?

You can write to your councillor about a local concern. You may also be able to share your views at a community council or committee meeting when a relevant item is open for public input.

Before contacting an official, note the location, the date, any photos or request numbers, and the outcome you want. Then follow the public decision: Was the proposal approved, changed, referred for more work or rejected?

A councillor’s job is to represent residents and participate in City decisions. Knowing where to report a problem—and how to check what happened afterward—helps Ward 7 residents make their concerns heard.
