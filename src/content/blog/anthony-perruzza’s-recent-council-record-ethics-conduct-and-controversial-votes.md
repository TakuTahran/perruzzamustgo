Anthony Perruzza has represented communities in northwest Toronto for many years, giving residents a lengthy public record to examine.

His recent record contains several different types of issues that should not be confused with one another.

## Ethics Finding

The most significant official finding is the 2024 Integrity Commissioner decision concerning election-period use of City resources.

Perruzza was found to have violated Article 15 of the Code of Conduct.

However, no penalty was recommended after he self-reported and mitigated the issue.

## Council Conduct

Separate Council records document confrontations involving Speaker Frances Nunziata and Councillor Paul Ainslie.

One meeting ended with Perruzza being removed from the chamber.

Another included an allegation by Ainslie concerning an obscene gesture, followed by an apology.

## Policy Votes

Perruzza has also taken controversial or unsuccessful positions regarding speed cameras, development applications and Toronto’s alcohol-in-parks program.

These votes represent policy disagreements—not ethics findings.

## Why the Distinction Matters

Responsible political reporting should not label every controversial vote a “scandal.”

Residents deserve to know precisely what happened.

A confirmed ethics violation should be described as one.

A procedural dispute should be identified as a procedural dispute.

A policy vote should be evaluated on its merits.

Making those distinctions produces a more credible assessment of Anthony Perruzza’s Ward 7 record.
