Public safety is rarely improved by a single meeting or one government program.

For Ward 7 — Humber River–Black Creek, the challenge of gun violence involves policing, youth opportunities, community services, education, housing and economic conditions.

Anthony Perruzza’s March 2024 Jane–Finch gun-violence roundtable created several specific areas that can now be examined.

## Visible Police Presence

One idea discussed at the meeting was stronger police visibility.

A useful accountability review would examine whether Toronto Police increased patrols or community engagement in identified areas and for how long those changes continued.

## Better Lighting

Improved lighting was another proposed response.

Lighting alone cannot eliminate violent crime, but urban-safety strategies often examine poorly illuminated public spaces, pathways, parking areas and transit connections.

Residents can therefore ask which Ward 7 locations received improvements following the meeting.

## Youth Outreach

Perhaps the most significant long-term issue is youth engagement.

Employment, recreation, mentorship and community programming may play important roles in violence prevention.

The roundtable identified direct youth outreach as an area requiring attention.

That creates a measurable question: what programs were actually expanded after March 2024?

## Accountability Without Exaggeration

There is a legitimate difference between asking whether government initiatives delivered results and blaming one councillor for individual crimes.

The first is public accountability.

The second requires evidence that normally will not exist.

For journalists and residents, focusing on commitments, funding, implementation and measurable outcomes provides a stronger and more credible assessment of Anthony Perruzza’s Ward 7 public-safety record.
