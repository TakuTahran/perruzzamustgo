For tenants dealing with no heat, leaking pipes, pests, broken doors or other apartment problems, one question matters immediately:

**How quickly will someone respond?**

Toronto already has rules setting response times for many apartment maintenance requests.

But getting a problem from the first complaint to an actual repair can involve several steps.

For Ward 7 tenants, understanding those steps can make it easier to document delays, escalate unresolved complaints and determine who is responsible for the next action.

## Start With a Written Repair Request

The first step for most apartment maintenance problems is to notify the landlord or property manager.

Toronto advises tenants to submit maintenance requests **in writing**.

A written request should clearly identify:

* The problem
* The exact location or unit
* When the problem began
* Whether it affects essential services
* Photographs, when useful
* The date the request was submitted

Tenants should keep a copy.

For buildings covered by RentSafeTO, landlords are also required to maintain records of tenant service requests and their responses.

This creates a clearer timeline if the issue remains unresolved.

## Urgent Problems Have a 24-Hour Response Requirement

Some maintenance issues are considered urgent because they involve vital services.

These include problems with:

* Electricity
* Gas
* Heat
* Water
* Building security

For RentSafeTO-covered apartment buildings, landlords or property owners are required to **respond to urgent service requests within 24 hours**.

That does not necessarily mean every complex repair must be fully completed within 24 hours.

It does mean the request should receive a response and action should begin according to the circumstances.

For tenants, the important information to record is:

**When was the problem reported?**

**When did the landlord respond?**

**What action was promised or taken?**

## Non-Urgent Requests Also Have a Timeline

Not every repair is an emergency.

Problems such as some minor damage or non-essential repairs may fall into the non-urgent category.

Under RentSafeTO requirements, landlords must respond to non-urgent service requests within **seven days**.

Again, tenants should keep the original written request and any reply.

A verbal conversation can be useful, but a dated written record makes it much easier to establish what was reported and when.

## What If the Landlord Does Not Resolve the Problem?

If the issue remains unresolved after the tenant has contacted the landlord, residents can contact **Toronto 311**.

A 311 complaint creates a City service request.

A bylaw enforcement officer may then contact the tenant and ask for additional information such as:

* Photographs
* Copies of maintenance requests
* Details about the condition
* Previous communication with the landlord

This is why documenting the issue from the beginning can make later follow-up easier.

## The City Also Has Response Targets

After a complaint is submitted through 311, RentSafeTO publishes response timelines for City follow-up.

For an **urgent complaint involving vital services**, City staff are expected to respond within approximately **24 hours**.

For a **non-urgent complaint**, the team aims to respond within approximately **five to 10 days**.

Tenants should save the 311 reference number they receive.

That number becomes an important part of the complaint history.

## Tenants Can Escalate a Complaint

Residents do not necessarily have to start over when a City response is delayed.

RentSafeTO provides an escalation process.

A tenant can contact 311 again when:

* An urgent complaint has not received a response from a bylaw enforcement officer within 24 hours, or
* A non-urgent complaint has not received a response within the City's five-to-10-day response window.

When a complaint is escalated, the City can provide information about the supervisor handling the case.

This gives tenants another formal step when the initial complaint process is not moving as expected.

## Enforcement Can Go Beyond a Phone Call

If City staff confirm a bylaw violation, RentSafeTO uses progressive enforcement.

Depending on the situation, enforcement can include:

* A Notice of Violation
* An Order to Comply
* Inspection or re-inspection activity
* Tickets or charges in appropriate cases
* Remedial action in some circumstances

Compliance timelines can vary depending on the type and urgency of the violation.

An urgent situation may require immediate action, while some non-urgent violations may have longer compliance periods.

That distinction is important.

A quick response to a complaint does not always mean the underlying repair will be completed on the same day.

## Better Tracking Can Help Tenants Follow Their Complaint

One of the simplest ways to improve the experience for tenants is to make each step easier to follow.

A tenant should be able to answer:

**When did I report the problem?**

**When did the landlord respond?**

**What repair was promised?**

**When did I contact 311?**

**What is my service-request number?**

**Has a City officer contacted me?**

**Was a violation found?**

**What is the deadline for compliance?**

A basic complaint tracker can look like this:

**Problem:**
**Date first noticed:**
**Landlord notified:**
**Landlord response:**
**Photos saved:**
**311 contacted:**
**311 reference number:**
**City response date:**
**Inspection completed:**
**Order or violation issued:**
**Repair deadline:**
**Current status:**

This turns a general complaint into a documented case.

## Building Owners Are Required to Track Service Requests

RentSafeTO-covered building owners are required to have a system for receiving, tracking and responding to tenant service requests.

They must also retain records of those requests.

This means repair complaints should not simply disappear after a tenant speaks with building management.

A documented system makes it possible to examine whether requests were received, how quickly they were answered and what action was taken.

## Building Information Is Becoming More Visible

Toronto has also introduced colour-coded RentSafeTO signs for qualifying apartment buildings.

The signs use three categories:

**Green – Satisfactory**

**Yellow – Conditional**

**Red – Action Required**

The colour reflects the building's most recent RentSafeTO evaluation score.

Buildings are required to display their current sign near the main entrance.

Residents can also look up building evaluations and certain active violations through City resources.

These tools can make building conditions more transparent, although a building-wide score does not describe every individual apartment.

## Faster Responses Require More Than Reporting

A faster system is not only about how quickly a tenant can submit a complaint.

Several stages matter:

**Fast acknowledgement**

Residents should know that their request has been received.

**Clear classification**

Tenants should know whether the issue is considered urgent or non-urgent.

**Visible deadlines**

Residents should know when the landlord or City is expected to respond.

**Easy escalation**

If a deadline passes, tenants should know exactly where to go next.

**Clear enforcement**

When a violation is confirmed, residents should be able to understand what the owner has been ordered to do and by when.

**Follow-up**

The process should confirm whether the underlying problem was actually corrected.

These are practical measures residents can use when evaluating whether the repair and complaint system is working effectively.

## What Could Be Improved?

There are several questions residents can raise when discussing ways to make apartment-maintenance responses faster.

Could tenants receive clearer status updates after filing complaints?

Could unresolved cases be automatically flagged when response deadlines pass?

Could residents more easily see whether an Order to Comply has been issued?

Could repeated complaints at the same building be identified more quickly?

Could staffing or inspection capacity affect response times?

Could tenants receive clearer information about what happens after an inspection?

These are questions that can be directed to City officials and councillor candidates when discussing housing and tenant services.

Any proposed change should also explain:

**Who would implement it?**

**Would additional staffing or funding be required?**

**What response-time target would change?**

**How would residents measure whether the change worked?**

## Not Every Rental Building Falls Under RentSafeTO

Residents should also know that RentSafeTO does not cover every type of rental housing.

The program applies to apartment buildings with **three or more storeys and 10 or more units**.

Condominiums, townhouses and rental units in private homes are not included in the RentSafeTO program.

However, Toronto's Property Standards Bylaw applies more broadly, and tenants with unresolved property-maintenance concerns can still contact 311 to determine the appropriate process.

## What Ward 7 Tenants Can Do Now

For tenants dealing with a repair problem, the practical process is:

**1. Document the problem**

Record the date, location and condition.

**2. Notify the landlord in writing**

Keep a copy of the request.

**3. Know the response timeline**

Urgent RentSafeTO requests require a response within 24 hours. Non-urgent requests require a response within seven days.

**4. Contact 311 if the problem remains unresolved**

Keep the reference number.

**5. Track the City's response**

Urgent RentSafeTO complaints have a 24-hour City response target, while non-urgent complaints generally have a five-to-10-day target.

**6. Escalate if the response window passes**

Contact 311 again and ask about escalation.

**7. Follow the repair itself**

A response or closed service request is not the same thing as confirming that the physical problem has been corrected.

## The Key Question Is Whether the Problem Gets Fixed

Response times matter.

But the final measure is not simply how quickly a landlord or City office replies.

The important question is:

**Was the problem actually resolved?**

A strong apartment-maintenance system should make it easy for tenants to report problems, understand deadlines, follow enforcement and see what happened after the complaint.
